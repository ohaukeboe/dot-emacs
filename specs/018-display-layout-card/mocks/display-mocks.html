Three ways to arrange displays in the Mango shell

Each mock uses the shell's own palette, radii and controls. Drag the displays and press the controls; they respond. The built-in panel is this laptop's. The Dell is an example second display.

Mock A

Floating card

A launcher-width card in the middle of the screen. The layout sits on top and the selected display's settings below. Apply asks to keep the change and reverts after ten seconds.

  • Opens from launcher or a keybind
  • Closest to wdisplays
  • Cost one new canvas, the rest is existing controls

Mock B

Quick-controls page

A detail page in the status popup, next to Wi-Fi and sound. No dragging: you place a display beside another with two choices, and a small picture confirms the result. Everything works from the keyboard.

  • Opens from a Displays row in quick controls
  • Cannot do free offsets or three-display layouts in one step
  • Cost smallest, no drag code

Mock C

Full-screen arrange

The whole screen becomes the canvas, so displays are large and easy to drag. One capsule at the bottom carries the selected display's settings. Each press steps to the next value.

  • Opens from a keybind, and when an unknown display is plugged in
  • Best for three or more displays
  • Cost a new full-screen surface; stepping through values is slower than picking
